# Intelligence

When we infer something from a piece of information that is not obvious and available to read by general readers, it is a sign that we are ourself doing something to the information at the time of reading. That is a double operation and needs something more to be performed. 

This process took a long, hard time for us to figure out and make it happen artificially. We could always do it of course, but for us to make it happen outside of us took a lot of effort. This has been possible only for the last few years. Now, we have artificial intelligence. But there are still problems left for us to solve. 

One, we still have to realise that intelligence is most strongly evidenced in the capability to learn and learn further on the basis of past learnings. Currently we use frozen artificial intelligence models and we don't know how far off continuously self-learning models are. Till we figure this out, we will not be able to realise the full potential of artificial intelligence because intelligence is staggered. 

Two, intelligence might actually not just be a cerebral thing. It might be closely connected to our body and its nervous system. Not just as a distributed inference system but as a source of data (input) and as a context for continual training. This doesn't mean artificial intelligence has to be necessarily bound to physical systems like that of robots. There could be systems imagined which use physical systems for input and base their intelligence on it. 

Three, today we live with the fear of artificial intelligence and do not have any good guard on the possibility of it going rogue.  We need to understand that intelligence is never purely artificial — the source of instincts (data points, the "keys") is still human. Intelligence would be truly artificial if the data used to train it to be able to infer would be artificial too. This is a strange thought because immediately we can see the fallacy of this line of thought. Intelligence might only be the context in which the inferences of a system seem intelligent. So, if it needs to serve the needs of humans and it has to "seem" intelligent to humans, the data has to be human as well. The data needs to point towards patterns that need to "seem" intelligent in whichever context. 

So there is no fundamentally "intelligent" activity. Activities only seem intelligent to whoever needs intelligence to be performed. 

And now we come upon a big problem. If intelligence is only about seeming and not about being, how do we figure that activities can seem intelligent without them having a stake in the matter in question? If,  something being intelligent is our perception only — what we perceive as intelligent is not *really* intelligent and it does not even need to be (even if it could!). There is nothing called real intelligence at all. 

This is a liberating thought and a very useful one. If we can figure how to perceive something as intelligent from somebody else's perspective, what would it mean? Is it even possible to do that? What does intelligence from some other entity's (person or thing) perspective even mean? It might even be that such a thing is not good for us. And something that we do not consider as desirable. One of the reasons for the times of social discord that we live in, is that we are not able to accept that some things might need to happen because they are in someone else's interest only and not in ours.
